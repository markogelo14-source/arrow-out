# What makes puzzle games popular on the App Store

Researched Oct 14, 2026. Primary sources (Apple, the makers of the games, and the companies that own the data) were used where they
could be reached. Every claim names its source inline. Secondary sources are marked **(secondary)**, and anything unconfirmed is
marked **(unverified)**. Market numbers come from different data firms whose methods differ, so compare them with care.

## TL;DR
- Puzzle is one of the two biggest mobile genres by player spending, and it leads by a wide margin in ad revenue. Most of its growth now comes from merge, sort, block and screw puzzles, not classic match-3.
- **Arrow puzzles are the hit format of 2026.** Sensor Tower's monthly charts put arrow escape games in the worldwide top 5 by downloads every month from April to August 2026, at No. 1 in May. The reasons it gives are simple rules, quick retries, flat visuals and short video ads that convert well. That same simplicity means many near-identical clones are now competing.
- The design traits behind the hits are well documented: short, readable levels; difficulty that is tuned but never brutal ("crazy hard levels never pay off", King); instant feel; and a daily habit loop (Wordle).
- Being found on the App Store comes down to the first 1 to 3 screenshots or the preview video, ratings (which Apple says affect search ranking), In-App Events, and Featuring Nominations to Apple's editors. Apple's editors look for uniqueness, accessibility, localization and polish, not download numbers.

## 1. Market facts (with dates)

### How big puzzle is
- **Player spending, 2025:** Mobile puzzle games made **over $10B in in-app purchase (IAP) revenue in 2025, up 14% year over year**. That made puzzle the #2 genre after Strategy. Puzzle downloads were over **9.7B**. Royal Match made about $1.4B and Candy Crush Saga about $1.1B. These are Sensor Tower figures, reported in [Naavik, "What Leading Match-3 & Merge Games Do Differently", Jan 18 2026](https://naavik.co/digest/what-leading-match-3-and-merge-games-do-differently/) **(secondary: analysis of Sensor Tower data)**.
  - A summary of Sensor Tower's *State of Gaming 2026* gives puzzle **$14.4B** and names it, with strategy, as a top growth genre ([GameDev Reports, Mar 19 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026), **secondary**). This does not match the $10B figure. The two probably define the genre differently, but I could not read the original PDF to check. **Treat the exact size as uncertain (somewhere between $10B and $14B).**
  - AppMagic, which counts IAP only and leaves out web shops and Android in China, puts casual puzzle at **$8.2B in 2025 (+7.6%) with downloads up 9.9%** ([Mobidictum summary of AppMagic Casual report 2025](https://mobidictum.com/appmagic-casual-games-report-2025-summary/), **secondary**).
- **Overall market, 2025:** Mobile game IAP revenue was $81.75B (+1.3%) and downloads were 50.41B (−7.2%). App Store game revenue was $52.5B. Non-game apps out-earned games for the first time. Source: Sensor Tower *State of Gaming 2026*, via [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026) **(secondary)**. iOS is where most of the money is.
- **Share of downloads:** Puzzle and Simulation each had about **20% of global game downloads in 2024**, and puzzle was down 3% (Sensor Tower *State of Mobile Gaming 2025*, as quoted in search results). **(unverified: I could not open the PDF.)**
- **Ad revenue:** In June 2026 Sensor Tower's own *Gaming Deep Dive: Ad Monetization Report* said puzzle titles take **"more than half of all ad earnings"** on most of the big ad networks ([Sensor Tower, Jun 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/gaming-deep-dive-ad-monetization-report)). Game World Observer's write-up of the same report gives more exact numbers: **puzzle had 53% of game ad revenue (Feb to Apr 2026)**, **Block Blast! made about $127M from ads (Jan to May 2026), the most of any game**, and **55.6% of games use ads (May 2026)** ([Game World Observer, Jul 2026](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=29151)). I could not find these exact figures on the public Sensor Tower page. **(secondary)**
- **Retention:** GameAnalytics names puzzle among the "best genres for medium and long-term retention". Across all genres, the median D7 retention is about 3.4 to 3.9%, and the top-quartile D1 is about 26 to 28%. The median session is 5 to 6 minutes, and the top quartile is 8 to 9 minutes ([GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks, via GameDev Reports, Feb 17 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks), **secondary**; [original PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf)). The puzzle-only retention numbers are in charts I could not read.

### Trends
- **The arrow puzzle wave (2026).** All of these come from Sensor Tower's own monthly worldwide top-10 posts (primary):
  - **April 2026:** Arrows Puzzle Escape and Arrows GO! were **No. 1 and No. 2 by download growth**, and No. 3 and No. 5 by total downloads. Sensor Tower: "Simple top-down controls, flat visuals, and instantly understandable mechanics made both titles easy to pick up… well suited for short sessions." ([Sensor Tower, Apr 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-april-2026))
  - **May 2026:** Arrows Puzzle Escape was the **No. 1 most downloaded mobile game in the world**, ahead of Block Blast!. Sensor Tower credits "simple visual rules, quick retry loops, and instantly understandable mechanics" and paid ads: the publisher "ramped up paid display activity", and the "ad-friendly format is easy to showcase in short video creatives." ([Sensor Tower, May 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-may-2026))
  - **June 2026:** Arrows Puzzle Escape was No. 3 and Arrows GO! No. 4, and Arrow Puzzle: Tap Puzzle Games was also in the top 10. Sensor Tower says the format lets "multiple competing titles … scale simultaneously." ([Sensor Tower, Jun 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-june-2026))
  - **August 2026:** Arrow Puzzle: Tap Puzzle Games was still in the worldwide top 5 by downloads ([Sensor Tower, Aug 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-august-2026)).
  - **The space is crowded.** Dozens of arrow escape apps came out in 2026, many with almost the same name ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6780872690), [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/arrows-flow-escape-puzzle/6761592557) listings, **secondary, estimates**). One example is *Arrows – Puzzle Escape* (Lessmore UG), released Aug 2025. It has a 4.7★ rating from 12.4K ratings, was #5 in Puzzle (top free) on Mar 12 2026, and sells hints, **streak freezers and streak fixers**, lives and remove-ads ([AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/6748397500), **secondary**).
- **Sort, block and screw are where the growth is, while match-3 has gone flat.** AppMagic says **Sort Puzzle revenue grew 116% in 2025**, and its revenue growth leaders were Block, Logic, Sort, Merge and Sudoku. Match-3 is still the largest casual subgenre, but its revenue did not grow. Casual D14 to D30 retention fell 3.5 to 5.4% ([AppMagic *Mobile Market Landscape 2026*](https://appmagic.rocks/research/mobile-landscape-report-2026), via [GameDev Reports, Feb 6 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape), **secondary**). In Sensor Tower data, Merge drove about 60% of puzzle's revenue growth in 2025, Sort 13%, Block 13% and Screw 8% (Naavik, above).
- **Hybrid-casual:** AppMagic's hypercasual/hybrid segment grew **IAP revenue 80% year over year (4 to 5x in three years)** while its downloads fell 3.7%. Block Puzzle was its fastest-growing subgenre. It makes money from **fail offers and small currency bundles (≤$8)** (AppMagic, same source). Deconstructor of Fun calls screw puzzles a "Hybridcasual Puzzle" leader. Screwdom made **over $75M in IAP in 2025 while still showing an interstitial ad after every level**. The genre's appeal is "fast readability, satisfying interaction, and a clean decision loop" ([Deconstructor of Fun, "The Screw Puzzle Gold Rush", Feb 20 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/the-screw-puzzle-gold-rush)).
- **Block Blast!** (Hungry Studio) was the only game in 2025's top downloads with **368M installs (+66%)** (AppMagic, above). Its studio says it has **70M daily users**, ran more than 10,000 A/B tests in 2025, and follows a "Less Is More" rule: no feature creep, and every update must serve the core loop ([Hungry Studio press release, Business Wire, Jan 8 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260108572322/en/Block-Blast-Scales-to-70M-DAU-as-Hungry-Studio-Runs-Over-10000-Experiments-in-a-Single-Year), primary, but the numbers are self-reported).

## 2. Core loop and design traits of the hits

- **Players understand it in one glance.** Sensor Tower explains every arrow or puzzle chart hit in 2026 the same way: "simple rules", "instantly understandable mechanics", "flat visuals", "quick retry loops" (Sensor Tower monthly posts, above). Deconstructor of Fun says the same about screw puzzles: "fast readability".
- **Short levels, and shorter still when they are hard.** King's data team at GDC 2024: "The longer the level is, the less likely it is to be fun", and harder levels should be shorter. Across all games, the median session is about 5 to 6 minutes (GameAnalytics, above). Source: [mobilegamer.biz report on King's GDC talk "Sugary Statistics", Mar 27 2024](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/) ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034392/Sugary-Statistics-What-We-Have)).
- **Difficulty is tuned, not punishing.** Candy Crush's level 65 earned the most revenue but also lost the most players. When King made it easier, players stayed longer. Jan Wedekind of King: **"crazy hard levels never pay off, at least in the long term… retention always wins."** King measures fun as "time to abandon" against "time to pass", and keeps fixing its 100 least fun levels. The least engaging levels were the hard, skill-based "puzzly" ones (same source).
- **A small mistake budget makes the stakes feel real.** Screw puzzles give "forgiving but short leash" slots, so one mistake is survivable but repeated ones end the level. Showing players what is coming next (an X-ray peek, the next box) turns guessing into planning (Deconstructor of Fun, above). Arrowly's 3 hearts and hint system do the same job.
- **Feel and tactility.** Screwdom's 3D design feels "like you are actually dismantling something" (Deconstructor of Fun). Apple's editors list "art and animation, controls… sound design and music, technical performance" among the things they judge games on ([Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)).
- **Polish over features.** Mini Metro's GDC 2017 talk was titled "When Less Is More" ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/attend-gdc-2017-and-learn-the-value-of-less-is-more-from-a-mini-metro-dev)), and Block Blast's rule is "Less Is More" (above).
- **Original ideas get copied fast.** Threes was cloned within weeks (1024, then 2048), and its makers spoke out publicly against the copies ([Game Informer, Mar 28 2014](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/03/28/threes-developer-speaks-out-against-flood-of-clones), **secondary**; the makers' original letter could not be retrieved). The same thing is happening to arrow puzzles in 2026.

## 3. Retention and habit mechanics

- **One daily puzzle, scarcity, and sharing (Wordle).** Josh Wardle said the breakthrough was **limiting play to one game per day**, which made it scarce. He wanted "something that encourages you to spend three minutes a day", and the game had "no ads and I am not doing anything with your data — and that is also quite deliberate" (Wardle in NYT "Wordle Is a Love Story", Jan 2022, as quoted by [AP via Boston.com, Jan 4 2022](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/), **secondary quote of a primary interview**). The **spoiler-free emoji share grid came from players**: people in New Zealand typed it out by hand, and Wardle then built it in, in December 2021 ([The Spinoff, Jan 13 2022](https://thespinoff.co.nz/media/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault), **secondary**).
- **Daily puzzles at scale.** NYT Games had about **11.2B puzzle solves in 2025**, about 4.2B of them Wordle. Streaks clearly matter to players: the 2025 recap noted that one Wordle answer ("Bongo") **broke 36,337 streaks** ([TODAY/NBC syndication, Dec 2025](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/6436464/), **secondary; I could not find NYT's own release**). NYT's Q2 2026 results do not break out games numbers ([NYT Q2 2026 release PDF](https://static.poder360.com.br/uploads/2026/08/New-York-Times-2Q26.pdf)).
- **Streaks are something players will pay for.** The top-charting arrow game sells "Streak Freezers" and a "Streak Fixer" (AppPricingLab, above).
- **Events and teasing what is coming.** App Annie (now data.ai) found that every one of the top 5 match-3 games for D30 retention used in-game events and teased upcoming content. Royal Match led with **16.5% D30 retention** (Q3 2021) ([PocketGamer.biz on App Annie report](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/dream-games-royal-match-leads-match-3-d30-retention-at-165/), **secondary; old data**). Current leaders add map-based "expedition" events, minigames and social PvP events (Naavik, above).
- **Retention in casual games is getting harder.** Casual D14 to D365 retention fell in 2025 (AppMagic, above), so a habit loop matters more than before.

## 4. Monetization models and trade-offs

| Model | Evidence | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Ads only / ads first** | Puzzle is more than 50% of game ad revenue. Block Blast! made about $127M from ads (Jan to May 2026) (Sensor Tower/GWO). Ads make up 70.3% of game revenue in India but only 22.1% in the US (same source). | Needs huge download volume, and in the US that means paying for ads. Interstitials hurt the feel. Requires ATT/SDK privacy disclosures, so Arrowly would lose its "Data Not Collected" label. Apple 2.5.18: interstitials must have visible close buttons and a way to report ads. |
| **Hybrid (ads + IAP)** | Screwdom made $75M+ IAP and also shows interstitials (DoF). Hybrid-casual IAP grew 80% (AppMagic). Candy Crush makes about $200M a year from rewarded ads (estimate in Naavik). | Most of the money comes from **fail offers**, which sell "relief from constraints" (DoF), and from small bundles. That pushes design toward making levels frustrating on purpose, which King's data warns hurts retention. |
| **IAP-led F2P** | Royal Match about $1.4B and Candy Crush about $1.1B in 2025 (Sensor Tower via Naavik). | Needs live ops, events and a meta layer, which is far more than a solo developer can run. |
| **Premium (paid)** | Monument Valley made back its costs in its first week, and sold 2.44M copies for $5.86M lifetime revenue (ustwo infographic, Jan 2015, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/15/monument-valley-team-reveals-the-cost-and-reward-of-making-a-hit-ios-game), **secondary**; old data). | A paid app gets very few installs. It works best for hand-crafted, art-led games that Apple features. |
| **Apple Arcade** | Apple invites pitches for "a fun, unreleased game". Arcade games have no ads or IAP ([Apple Arcade dev page](https://developer.apple.com/apple-arcade/)). | Arrowly is about to be released, so it would no longer count as unreleased. Deal terms are not public. **(unverified)** |
| **Free, no monetization (Arrowly v1)** | Wordle grew with no ads and no data collection, then sold for low seven figures (Jan 2022, [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/01/31/new-york-times-wordle-game-acquisition)). | Earns nothing. It does keep the product page and privacy label clean, which is a selling point. |

App Store rules that apply here ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)):
- **3.1.1:** Unlocking levels, hints or currencies has to go through Apple's In-App Purchase, and purchased currencies may not expire.
- **3.2.2(x):** An app may reward players for "completing a level, watching an ad", but must not force a rating or review.
- **4.3(b):** Apple rejects apps "indistinguishable from what's already widely available". This matters for a genre full of clones.

## 5. Discoverability

**Product page** ([Apple, Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)):
- The name and subtitle can each be up to 30 characters. Promotional text (170 characters) can be changed without a new build, but it does **not** affect search ranking. Keywords are limited to 100 characters, and Apple says not to repeat plurals, the category name or "app" in them.
- Search results show "the first one to three images… when no app preview is available, so make sure these highlight the essence of your app." App previews can be up to 30 seconds long, up to 3 per page, and **autoplay muted**, so "make sure the first few seconds… are visually compelling."
- **"Ratings and reviews influence how your app ranks in search."**
- **Product Page Optimization:** you can test up to 3 versions of the icon, screenshots or preview against the original for up to 90 days ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/)).
- **Custom Product Pages:** you can publish up to 70 extra versions of the page, and **each can be assigned its own keywords** so it shows up in search for those terms ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)).

**Ratings prompts** (Apple [HIG: Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ratings-and-reviews), [requestReview(in:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/skstorereviewcontroller/requestreview(in:)), [Requesting App Store reviews](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requesting-app-store-reviews)):
- Ask only after players have shown they are engaged, "for example… when they complete a game level". Never ask on first launch, during onboarding, in the middle of play, or from a button tap.
- The system shows the prompt **at most 3 times per 365 days**. Someone who already rated the app is asked again only after a new version **and** more than 365 days. The prompt never appears in TestFlight builds.
- Apple's sample code waits until the user has completed the task at least 4 times and has not been prompted for the current version. Apple suggests a week or two between prompts. A permanent "Rate" link (`?action=write-review`) in Settings is allowed.

**In-App Events** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/)): event cards show up on the product page, **in search results**, and in editors' and personalized picks on the Today and Games tabs. The badges include Challenge, Competition and Live Event. An event can last up to 31 days and be promoted up to 14 days before it starts. Up to 10 can be published at once, and they are reviewed without a new build.

**Featuring by Apple's editors** ([Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)):
- "No checklist". Apple considers UX, UI design, innovation, **uniqueness** ("a fresh approach to a familiar category"), **accessibility**, **localization**, and the product page. For games it adds gameplay, art and animation, controls, replayability, sound, performance and value.
- Nominate through **Featuring Nominations** in App Store Connect with at least 2 weeks' notice, or **up to 3 months** ahead to be considered more widely.
- Recent award winners show which puzzle games Apple rewards: logic puzzles with a strong personality. *Is This Seat Taken?* won the 2026 Apple Design Award for Delight and Fun as "a fun way to experience and solve tricky logic puzzles", and *Blue Prince* won for Innovation ([Apple Newsroom, Jun 2 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-reveals-winners-of-the-2026-apple-design-awards/)). *Art of Fauna* ("approachable puzzles") was a 2025 App Store Awards Cultural Impact finalist ([Apple Newsroom, Nov 19 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/11/apple-announces-finalists-for-the-2025-app-store-awards/)). Monument Valley's sales went up after its 2014 Apple Design Award ([Engadget, Aug 11 2014](https://www.engadget.com/2014-08-11-monument-valley-recouped-costs-in-one-week.html), **secondary**).

**Paid ads drive the charts.** Sensor Tower credits the arrow puzzles' rise to paid ads, and says puzzle is a genre where ad spend share is higher than IAP revenue share in almost every market (May 2026 post; State of Gaming 2026 via GameDev Reports). A free game with no ads and no ad budget cannot buy its way onto the charts the way the arrow games did. Its realistic routes are **search, featuring and word of mouth**.

## Implications for Arrowly

1. **The timing is right, but players will see it as a clone first.** Arrow escape is the most downloaded puzzle format of 2026 (Sensor Tower, Apr to Aug), and the field is crowded. Apple's guideline 4.3(b) and its editors' "uniqueness" criterion both reward something that is visibly different. Lead the product page with what sets Arrowly apart: **the same Daily Puzzle for every player, no ads, no timers, "Data Not Collected", and every level guaranteed solvable.** The chart leaders sell lives, remove-ads and streak repair, so these are real differences.
2. **Make screenshot 1 (or the first 3 seconds of a preview) show an arrow sliding off a board.** Apple shows the first 1 to 3 images in search, previews autoplay muted, and Sensor Tower says the format sells itself in short video. Adding an app preview should be moved up from "can come in an update" (`appstore/screenshots-and-icon.md`).
3. **Add `requestReview` after a win.** Nothing in the game code currently asks for a rating. Ask from the win card after, say, the 4th cleared level or a 3-day Daily streak, never on launch and never from a button. Ratings affect search ranking, and the limit is 3 prompts a year. Also add a "Rate Arrowly" link in Settings using `?action=write-review`.
4. **Keep levels short and the difficulty gentle.** King: harder levels should be shorter, and "crazy hard levels never pay off." Check that the jump from 4x4 to 8x10 boards does not create long, punishing boards. Consider removing hearts in early chapters, or tracking (on device only) how long players take before quitting a level.
5. **Make the daily habit stronger, Wordle-style.** Arrowly already has a shared Daily Puzzle, a 7-day streak and a Share button. Make the shared result a **spoiler-free emoji grid** (hearts, hints, time), since that is what spread Wordle. Consider a free streak "freeze" as goodwill, because competitors sell it.
6. **Add sound, and test the feel on a device.** The code has haptics but no audio (no `AVAudio*`). Apple's editors list sound design and "art and animation" for games, and "satisfying, tactile" is what the arrow, screw and sort hits are known for.
7. **Go for featuring, not paid installs.** Submit a Featuring Nomination in App Store Connect 2 weeks to 3 months before launch. Accessibility (VoiceOver labels and Reduce Motion are already partly done) and **localization** are both on Apple's list. Translating the listing and the UI into a few big markets costs little.
8. **Use In-App Events for discovery.** A recurring "Weekend Challenge" or "Daily Puzzle streak week" Challenge event appears in search and on the Today and Games tabs, and needs no new build.
9. **Keyword plan:** Custom Product Pages can each have their own keywords, so in a later version you could make pages for "tap away", "arrow escape" and "logic puzzle" searches. Promotional text does not affect ranking, so do not use it for keywords.
10. **Monetization later, if ever:** adding ads would cost Arrowly its privacy label and its "no ads" message, and ads only pay at Block Blast scale. If money matters later, a one-time "Supporter" IAP, or paid themed level packs and arrow skins (rule 3.1.1, IAP), keeps the calm, no-ads promise. Hybrid fail offers make money, but King's data says hard pay-walls cost retention, and they would clash with Arrowly's "calm" positioning.
